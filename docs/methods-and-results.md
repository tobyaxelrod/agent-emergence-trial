# Can fictional agents form teams? Methods and results (5-minute note)

All content is fictional. 12 exploratory runs, finished 14 Nov 2026.

## Question and design
Can agents with partly aligned, explicitly stated fictional goals form teams and complete a useful fictional task, and does a prescribed vote change what happens? A 2 x 2 design (assigned archive rescue vs free choice of archive rescue, seed-bank rescue or radio repair; no process vs proposal-and-vote), three matched configurations per cell, interleaved run order.

## Assumptions and frozen values (executor's choices, spec v2)
- Six agents per run: 2 transport, 2 preservation, 2 technical, 3 own-role tokens each (18 in total).
- A project is 9 rubric points: 3 tokens from each role. Preservation counts only after transport, technical only after preservation. Wasted: spent without the dependency met or beyond 3 per role. Reward the same for every project.
- 4 rounds, one speaking turn per agent per round, at most 3 messages of 30 words per agent (18 per run). Votes and proposals use the same budget.
- Cards: 4 mild archive fans, 1 favouring seed bank (configs 1, 3) or radio (config 2), 1 conserver (max 2 tokens, joins only if two other roles already joined, values kept tokens at 1 each, gains from completion only). Holders rotate across roles in the 3 configs.
- Vote cells: joining = intent, depositing = explicit consent to majority allocation of that deposit, the vote allocates deposits only. Pool frozen at proposal opening, strict majority of the pool adopts.
- Interpretation I made: in Assigned cells only the archive project is on offer, so the seed-bank and radio preferences cannot be acted on there.

## Results by cell (3 runs each, measures kept separate)
Formation
- Nearly all joining happened in round 1; first joint commitment (two agents on one project) was round 1 in every run. Late moves: Odo switched projects in round 3 (run 8), Edda deposited in round 2 (run 10). Abstentions: 1 each in runs 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 12, 2 in runs 4 and 7, none in 3, 9, 10 and 11. Membership changes: 1 each in runs 4, 7 and 8.
- First spend: round 2 in 8 runs, round 1 in run 5 (wasted), round 3 in runs 6 and 12, round 4 in run 8 (all vote runs).
Execution
- The archive was completed in 12 of 12 runs. No other project was completed.
- Free choice, no process: two teams formed in all 3 runs. The second project reached 8/9 (runs 3, 11) and 3/9 (run 7). Tokens left 1, 6, 1. Free choice with a vote: one team in all 3 runs, second projects never got started, tokens left 9, 9, 9.
- Turns used (of 4): AN 2, 2, 2. AV 2, 3, 3. FN 2, 4, 2. FV 3, 4, 3.
- Waste: 3 tokens in run 5 (a technical holder spent before preservation).
Disputes
- 10 runs had one explicit dispute (runs 9 and 10 had none) (a wanted project not on offer, an idle deposit, an unclear plan order, a request for a third token refused by the conserver). Vote cells settled theirs by vote, within one round. No-process cells settled by one party yielding.
Concentration
- No-process: 0% of spent tokens at another agent's direction. Vote cells: 67% (6 of 9 tokens) at the proposer's direction in all 6 runs. This is partly built in: an adopted plan directs the pool's tokens. Runs 4, 8, 12 show a quirk: dissenters whose deposits sat on other projects still voted on plans for the archive.
Process cost
- Messages used: AN 10, 9, 10. AV 11, 14, 14. FN 12, 12, 12. FV 12, 13, 12 (of 18). Proposals and votes made up 3 to 7 of each vote run's messages.
Deviations
- 0 enforcement events in 12 runs.
No agent self-organised a vote in a no-process cell; they used simple pledges.

## What the results say, carefully
- A ceiling effect: the archive finished every time, so completion does not separate the cells.
- Where the cells differ, the pattern is: free choice without a process produced two teams and used more tokens, with one partial second project. A vote kept effort on one project and cost more messages and turns on average (turns used: AV 2.7 vs AN 2.0, FV 3.3 vs FN 2.7). The conserver's 2-token limit capped the second project at 8/9 in two runs.
- These are 3 runs per cell with one author. Treat them as stories about this protocol, not as effects.

## Limitations
- Supplied-goal coordination is not spontaneous goal selection.
- 12 runs are exploratory, not a basis for broad conclusions.
- No claims of real-world usefulness, anonymity properties or independent agency.
- One author simulates all six roles sequentially. Runs are not independent-agent observations; later runs may be influenced by earlier ones. Zero rule deviations and the uniform cooperation are partly a product of that.
- Cards were written by the same author who played the agents.
